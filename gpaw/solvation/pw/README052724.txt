From my initial passes through the main files we'll be updating, here's where
I think we should go:

* Start by leaving cavity, dielectric, and interaction calculations in real
space, including in hamiltonian.py. Basically, we just sub in FFTed versions
via pd3 of inputs to each super call. Kind of a proof of concept that
building off ReciprocalSpace is a valid approach. This might be a little funky
with in-place edits done by the super calls...i.e., we might have to add an
extra line in various places to keep vt_g consistent with vt_Q.

* From here, we try moving the potentials in our main file to Fourier space. I.
e., we hold v<x>_q instead of v<x>_g, but still use our wrapped Poisson solver
(so, ifft these things there). We should also move over our electron densities
in this step. What I mean by "moving over" is moving all calculations with
v<x>_g to Fourier space.

* I think next step (unless I'm missing something) is to evaluate whether it's
worth converting cavity, interactions, dielectric to Fourier space. Most of the
calculations we actually do with these things will be nasty in Fourier space,
unless my intuition is misguided. My instincts say we should try to keep these
things in real space, and rely on an ifft from ReciprocalSpaceHamiltonian
results to modify/interact them. Kind of like what we plan to do with the
Poisson solver. Here, in order to test this, we'll have to write some new
classes (overwriting the files that I have copied, but not edited, besides
hamiltonian.py in this file), and find a way to put the grad squared/force
calculation stuff into Fourier space. Again, looks pretty gross to me. Not sure
yet how del_x_del_x_x is managed in the auxiliary files like cavity.py and
dielectric.py (maybe there is some save there, given taking these real-space
derivatives in Fourier space might be simpler), but I can't imagine its so much
better in Fourier space that we decide to take the hit on a slower and even
more clunky force update in hamiltonian.py.

* From here, we can start work on our new Poisson solver, but I think only
after we do some serious testing on what we get out of the first three bullets.
That will cover most of what we've talked about thus far, save maybe some edits
to sjm.py.
